# Code of conduct

## What this asks for

Argue with the work. Say what is wrong with a change, a number, a claim or a
document, as directly as you like, and expect the same in return. A review that
softens a real objection into a suggestion has failed at the only thing a review
is for.

Do not argue with the person. Somebody who made a mistake made a mistake; that
is the whole of what it says about them. The distinction is not a matter of tone,
and a sentence about a contributor rather than about their work is out of place
here however politely it is phrased.

Assume the other person is trying to get it right. Most of what reads as
carelessness is a missing piece of context, and asking for it costs one sentence.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment of any kind. Personal attacks, insults, or comments about a
contributor rather than their contribution. Comments demeaning anybody for who
they are. Publishing somebody's private information. Persistent unwelcome contact
after being asked to stop. Deliberately derailing a discussion so that a change
cannot be evaluated.

Sustained bad faith counts even where no single message crosses a line. So does
using this repository's tracker, pull requests or discussions to conduct a
dispute that is not about this project.

## Where this applies

Everywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, commit messages, review
comments, discussions, and any space where somebody is representing this project.

## Reporting

Write to <nils.lehnen@proton.me>. State what happened, where, and when, and
include links if there are any.

Reports are read by the maintainer and by nobody else. Nothing you send is
shared with the person reported without your agreement, unless there is no way to
act on the report without doing so, in which case you are told before it happens
and can withdraw the report instead.

If your report is about the maintainer, there is no second person here to send it
to. That is the honest position of a single-maintainer project and it is written
down rather than left to be discovered. GitHub's own reporting route exists
independently of this repository and is the escalation available in that case.

## What happens after a report

The maintainer decides, and the decision is one of: no action, with the reason;
a private request to change something; an edit or removal of the content; a
temporary block; or a permanent block from this repository.

A decision is communicated to the person who reported and to the person
reported. Where content is removed, the fact that something was removed is not
concealed, though its content is not republished.

## The limits of this document

This is a statement of what is expected, enforced by one person reading reports.
No mechanism in this repository refuses a message that breaks it, and none could:
the tracker and the pull requests are outside everything the workflows here read.
Treat it as what it is, an agreement between people, and not as a control.
